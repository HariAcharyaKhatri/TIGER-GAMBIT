Rules & Strategy

Learn how to command the four prowling tigers and twenty courageous goats in traditional Nepali Bagh-Chal.

Rule 1

The Sacred Alquerque Grid

Bagh-Chal is played on a 5×5 grid with 25 intersections. All pieces stand directly on the intersection points. Lines connect points horizontally, vertically, and along specific diagonals (where (row + column) is even).

💡 Key note: Not all points have diagonals! Only intersections with even coordinates share diagonal connections. The central point (C3) connects in all 8 directions.
Rule 2

The Four Corner Tigers

The four tigers begin stationed at the four corners of the board (A1, E1, A5, E5). A tiger can:

  • Step: Move one step along any connected line to an adjacent vacant intersection.
  • Pounce & Capture: Leap over an adjacent goat into an immediately vacant spot along a straight connected line. The jumped goat is captured and removed permanently.
Rule 3

The Twenty Goats: Placement & Movement

Goats start off the board and enter one by one during the Placement Phase. Goats always take the first turn of the match.

  • During placement, goats cannot move — they can only be placed on an empty intersection.
  • Once all 20 goats have been placed on the board, the Movement Phase begins.
  • In the movement phase, goats can move 1 step along any connected line to an adjacent empty intersection. Goats cannot capture tigers.
Rule 4

Victory & Draw Conditions

🐅 Tigers Win:

By capturing 5 goats. With 5 goats removed, the remaining herd cannot trap all tigers.

🐐 Goats Win:

By completely trapping all 4 tigers so that none of the tigers can make any legal step or leap.
